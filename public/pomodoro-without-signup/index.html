A pomodoro timer with no signup

Most pomodoro apps want an email address before you can start a 25-minute session. Adwait doesn't. Open the page, pick a task, press start — your tasks and sessions stay in your browser.

Why do pomodoro apps ask you to sign up?

Because their data lives on a server, and a server needs a way to know whose data is whose. An account is the standard answer: it enables sync, billing, and analytics, and it gives the company a way to email you. That's a reasonable trade for some products. For a timer, it's mostly friction — you wanted to start working, not manage a login.

Adwait takes the other route. There is no database and no API. The app is a static bundle; your tasks, buckets, and focus sessions are written to this browser's local storage. There's nothing to sign into because there's no "you" on our side.

What you get without an account

  • Focus, short break, and long break modes on the standard 25 / 5 / 15 cycle.
  • Start, pause, resume, and cancel — cancelled time is logged as cancelled, not counted as focus.
  • A task list beside the timer so the session attaches to a specific piece of work.
  • Colour-coded due dates: red for overdue, amber for today, green for later.
  • Time-taken per task, rolled up from completed sessions.
  • JSON export and import, so "no server" doesn't mean "no backup".

Does the timer survive a refresh or a sleeping laptop?

Yes, and it's the part most timers get wrong. Adwait doesn't decrement a counter — it stores the timestamp when the session ends and recomputes the remainder every 250 ms. Refreshing, switching tabs, or closing the lid doesn't drift the timer. If a session finishes while the tab is closed, it's recorded exactly once the next time you open the app.

The honest catch

Local-first means one browser, not every device. There's no sync between your laptop and your phone, and clearing site data will clear your tasks. That's why export exists: download a JSON backup, keep it somewhere you trust, and import it when you move. If you need automatic sync across devices, a cloud app is the better fit — and Adwait isn't pretending otherwise.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no trial, no paid tier, no email capture.

Do I need to install anything?

No. It's a web app. There's no native app and no extension.

Can I change the 25 / 5 lengths?

Not yet. The timer uses 25-minute focus blocks, 5-minute short breaks, and a 15-minute long break after every four focus sessions. Making the lengths adjustable is on the roadmap, not in the app today.

What happens to my data if I clear my browser?

It's gone, unless you exported a backup. Export first, or at least before clearing site data.
